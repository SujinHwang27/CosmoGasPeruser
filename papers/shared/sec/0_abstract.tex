\begin{abstract}
The low-redshift Lyman-$\alpha$ forest flux power spectrum $P_F(k)$ responds to
galactic and AGN feedback, but the question of \emph{which} feedback recipes a
given observable can tell apart, and at \emph{what} data volume, has not been
quantified. We measure pairwise distinguishability across the four matched
initial-condition feedback recipes of the Sherwood simulation suite at
$z\approx\zsnap$ --- no feedback, stellar wind, wind${+}$AGN, and
wind${+}$strong-AGN --- using $P_F(k)$ of stacked sightlines and a dedicated
binary random-forest classifier per recipe pair. We report three results. First,
a pairwise distinguishability lattice: at a stacking depth of $M=64$ sightlines,
\latClearedTotal{} of the \latClearedTotal{} recipe pairs not involving the
stellar-wind/wind${+}$AGN comparison separate above a balanced-accuracy floor of
$\latQualBar$ on the $16$th-percentile edge, and the strong-AGN recipe is
detectable against the other three pooled at $\latCdRest$. Second, an
$M$-scaling result: distinguishability rises monotonically with stacking depth,
giving a concrete data-volume requirement for each pair. Third, a controls
methodology --- a within-class self-pair null, a mean-flux-only baseline, and an
injection-recovery sensitivity calibration --- that lets a weak signal be claimed
honestly. With these controls we revise a result previously read as a null: the
stellar-wind versus wind${+}$AGN pair, indistinguishable per sightline, becomes a
weak but real $P_F(k)$-shape signal that emerges only with stacking
($M\geq\hbcMmin$), reaching $\hbcMtwofivesix$ median balanced accuracy at $M=256$
(p16 $\hbcMtwofivesixPsixteen$; gate-marginal). All results are conditional on
fixed cosmology, ultraviolet background, and thermal history within a single
Sherwood realization; no nuisance marginalization is performed. We do not claim
per-sightline feedback identification at $z\approx\zsnap$ from $P_F(k)$ alone.
\end{abstract}
